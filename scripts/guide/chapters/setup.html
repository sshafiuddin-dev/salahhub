Any tablet, phone, computer or smart TV browser from the last few years can become a prayer clock that stays on all day. Setting it up takes about ten minutes.

What you need

  • An Android tablet, an Amazon Fire tablet or an iPad.
  • Wi-Fi. The app checks the nearby masjid’s iqama times about every hour and the weather every 15 minutes, and installs new versions of itself.
  • A charger, and a spot near a power socket with a stand or wall mount.

Choose your device

Pick yours to see only its steps.

Android

  1. Open the app. In Chrome, go to iqamahub.app
  2. Add it to the home screen. Tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app. From now on, open IqamaHub from its icon. It opens without the address bar.
  3. Keep the screen on. In the app, tap Keep screen on at the bottom right of the screen (on a phone, the bulb at the top right).
  4. Set a long screen timeout as a backup. Settings › Display › Screen timeout, and choose the longest time.
  5. Optional: lock the device to the app. App pinning stops children leaving the app. Turn it on under Settings › Security › App pinning (on some tablets it is under Security & privacy), then pin IqamaHub from the recent apps screen.
Screen timeout is under Display. The longest choice differs from tablet to tablet.

Amazon Fire

  1. Open the app. In Silk Browser, go to iqamahub.app
  2. Add it to the home screen. Tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home Screen. Name it IqamaHub and tap Add.
  3. Keep the screen on. In the app, tap Keep screen on at the bottom right of the screen (on a phone, the bulb at the top right). On a Fire tablet this matters most: newer models have no “Never” choice for the screen timeout.
  4. Set a long screen timeout as a backup. Settings › Display › Display Sleep, and choose the longest time. On some models the menu is called Display & Sounds, and the setting is called Sleep.
Display Sleep is called Sleep on some Fire tablets.

iPad

  1. Open the app. In Safari, go to iqamahub.app
  2. Add it to the home screen. Tap the Share button ⬆, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Keep the screen on. In the app, tap Keep screen on at the bottom right of the screen (on a phone, the bulb at the top right).
  4. Stop the iPad locking itself. On older iPads, or if the screen still turns off: Settings › Display & Brightness › Auto-Lock › Never.
  5. Optional: lock the iPad to the app. Guided Access stops children leaving the app. Turn it on under Settings › Accessibility › Guided Access and set a passcode. Open IqamaHub, triple-click the top button (or the Home button, if your iPad has one), and tap Start. To leave, triple-click again and enter the passcode.
Auto-Lock set to Never keeps the iPad awake even when the app cannot.

The buttons at the bottom right

On a tablet these sit at the bottom right of every screen, at the end of the row of tabs. On a phone they are the three buttons at the top right.

The bottom right of the Home screen.
  1. Brightness changes how bright the page is: Bright, Soft, Dim or Darkest. Chapter 6 shows how to dim it by itself at night.
  2. Keep screen on stops the device going to sleep while the app is open (on a phone the button says Screen on). Tap it again to turn it off.
  3. Full screen shows the large clock and today’s times with nothing else around them. Tap Exit at the top right to come back. While full screen is open the screen stays on; tap Screen on there to let it sleep.

Help, in the row of tabs along the bottom (or under More when they do not all fit), opens this guide at the chapter for the screen you are on.

On every device

  • Tap the screen once after opening the app. Browsers only play sound after the page has been touched. If you see Tap to enable sound, tap it.
  • Turn the volume up, and check the device is not muted or on Do Not Disturb.
  • Keep it plugged in. If your device has a battery-protect or optimised-charging setting, turn it on. It stops the battery sitting at 100% all day.

Good to know

  • Alerts and Keep screen on only work while IqamaHub is open on the screen. Leave it open.
  • Will it run on my old tablet? If the clock appears and the seconds tick, yes. If the app says the browser cannot keep the screen on, use the screen timeout or Auto-Lock setting above instead.
