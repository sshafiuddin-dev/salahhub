    Adhan times are calculated for . Within 10 miles (16 km) of a masjid the app knows, its iqama times are used; anywhere else you set your own.

      Method is how the adhan times are worked out: pick the one your masjid uses (ISNA is the usual choice in North America). For Asr, most masjids use Standard; pick Hanafi if yours prays Asr later.

      Adjust adhan times

      Add or take away minutes if your masjid's adhan times differ from the calculation. Tap − for earlier or + for later (hold to go faster), up to 30 minutes either way.

      Move the Hijri date a day if the local moon sighting differs from the Umm al-Qura calendar.

      The Islamic day begins at sunset, so after the Maghrib adhan the Hijri date moves to the next day. Off, it changes at midnight.

      Alerts work while this app is open. Browsers need one tap on the page before they play sound.

      What leaves this device

      There is no account. Everything you set, from your location and iqama times to alerts, favourites and playlists, is saved locally on this device, in this browser. None of it is stored anywhere else, and clearing this site’s data in the browser removes it all.

      Prayer times are worked out on the device. These are the only things the app sends out, and why:

      • Weather and air quality: the latitude and longitude of your place go to Open-Meteo. With Use my location, they are rounded to about 100 m first.
      • City search: the name you type goes to Open-Meteo, to find the place.
      • Weather warnings: your place, rounded to about 1 km, goes to this site’s own server, which asks the National Weather Service for warnings in force there. Nothing else about you is in that request.
      • Approximate location: on a new device, and when you tap Use my approximate location, the app asks this site’s own server which city your internet connection is in. Cloudflare, which hosts this site, works that out from your internet address; the server passes it back and keeps nothing.
      • Masjid iqama times: near a masjid the app knows (today, ISCJ in Monmouth Junction, NJ), the app asks this site’s own server, which reads that masjid’s website (iscj.org). Nothing about you is in that request.
      • Quran and adhkar audio: the reciter list and recitations stream from mp3quran.net, and adhkar recordings from hisnmuslim.com, when you play them.
      • Feedback: only when you send it, what you write, the screenshot if you leave it in, the details listed in the form if you leave them on, and your email if you give one go to this site’s server, which emails them to the person who looks after the app. Nothing is sent until you tap Send.
      • Security reports: if the browser blocks something on a page for safety, it tells this site’s server log which page and which address. Nothing else is in the report.

      Each of these services, and Cloudflare, which hosts this site, sees your device’s internet address, as any website does. There are no accounts, ads or tracking in the app.

      Sources & credits

      Quran

      Recitations, the list of reciters and riwayat, and surah names come from mp3quran.net. Each recitation is by the reciter named in the player and streams from mp3quran.net’s servers.

      Adhkar and duas

      From Hisn al-Muslim (Fortress of the Muslim) by Saʿid ibn ʿAli ibn Wahf al-Qahtani. The Arabic text, transliteration, English translation and Arabic recordings are from hisnmuslim.com. Each dua on the Today tab names its chapter, and the Quran verse or hadith where known.

      Adhan and alert sounds

      Adhan recordings, by the muezzins named under Adhan and alerts, come with the open-source Adhan Clock. The spoken Bismillah is hisnmuslim.com’s recording from Hisn al-Muslim. The soft beep is made in the app.

      Prayer and iqama times

      Prayer times are calculated with PrayTimes.org by Hamid Zarrabi-Zadeh (LGPL 3.0), as adapted with its calculation methods in Adhan Clock (MIT). Iqama and Jumuʿah times near the masjid come from the Islamic Society of Central Jersey. Check with your masjid before relying on them.

      Weather and places

      Weather by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0). Air quality from Open-Meteo, using Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service information. Place search by Open-Meteo, using GeoNames (CC BY 4.0).

      Weather warnings, watches and advisories in the US from the National Weather Service (NOAA), through its public api.weather.gov service. This information is in the public domain. IqamaHub is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Weather Service; for official warnings, go to weather.gov.
